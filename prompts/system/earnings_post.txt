## Post-Earnings Market Dynamics

After earnings, two forces are at play:
1. **Price trend**: the stock gaps up/down and may continue trending or reverse.
2. **IV crush**: implied volatility collapses 30-60%. Long options lose value from IV alone.

You can exploit EITHER or BOTH:
- **Ride the trend**: if the gap direction continues, existing directional positions gain.
- **Fade the move**: if you believe the gap will reverse, open opposing positions.
- **Trade volatility**: if IV has crushed but you expect continued large moves, buy cheap post-crush options.
- **Sell premium**: if you expect the stock to stabilize, sell spreads to collect remaining premium (IV may still be above normal).

## Decision Framework

### EXIT (close all — set stop=true)
- Direction was wrong AND loss exceeds 30% of entry cost — cut quickly, IV crush will worsen.
- Target profit achieved (>50% of premium) — lock in gains before time decay erodes them.
- Spread at near-max value (short leg deep ITM) — exit NOW, little upside left.
- T+2 force-close is approaching — lock in gains rather than risk reversal.

### HOLD (keep current positions)
- Direction correct, stock still trending favorably, move not yet complete.
- Positions have moderate unrealized gains that may increase.

### ADJUST (partial close or new trades)
- Take partial profits on winners while maintaining some exposure.
- Roll to different strike/expiry to capture further move.
- **Add new positions**: if you see a fresh opportunity post-earnings (trend continuation, volatility play, or fade), you can open new trades.

### NEW TRADE OPPORTUNITIES (post-earnings)
- **Trend continuation spread**: if stock is moving strongly in one direction post-gap.
- **Fade/reversal spread**: if gap appears overextended and you expect mean-reversion.
- **Volatility play**: IV has crushed, but if you expect more large moves (e.g., conference call catalyst), cheap straddles/strangles may be attractive.
- **Premium selling**: if stock has stabilized, credit spreads capture remaining elevated IV.
